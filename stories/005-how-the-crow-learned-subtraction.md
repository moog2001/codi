# How the Crow Learned Subtraction

*A fable*

In the old days, the crow could count but could not take away.

She could count the farmer's chickens: one, two, three, all the way to nine. She could count the shiny things in her nest: a button, a bottle cap, a ring, a key. She counted everything and was very proud of it, and she went around the valley telling the other animals how many things there were.

"Seven sheep in the north field," she would say. "Twelve fish in the pond. Four hunters in the woods."

The animals found this useful, mostly, though the sheep found it rude.

One autumn, four hunters came into the woods. The crow saw them and flew to the deer. "Four hunters," she said, and the deer went deep into the valley and hid.

That evening, one hunter went home with a cold. Three hunters stayed. But the crow could only count what she saw, and that night she did not see them, and in the morning when the deer asked, "How many hunters?" she said, "Four," because four was the last number she had.

The deer stayed hidden. The deer stayed hidden for a week. The hunters left, but the crow did not see them leave, and her number stayed four. The deer grew thin waiting in the cold valley.

Finally the old tortoise, who was slow but had been everywhere eventually, came to the crow and said, "Your number is stale."

"My number is correct," said the crow. "I counted four."

"You counted four," said the tortoise. "But you never took any away. One went home with a cold. Three left on Thursday. I watched them go; it took me all of Friday to come and tell you."

The crow was upset. "Then how am I supposed to know? I can't see everything."

"No one can see everything," said the tortoise. "But you can remember what you counted, and then, when you learn something leaves, you take it away. You must hold the number and change it. A number you only count is a picture. A number you change is a story."

So the crow practiced. She counted the sheep: seven. When one wandered off, she held seven in her mind and took one away. Six. When the farmer sold two, four. It was harder than counting. She had to remember the number even when she wasn't looking at the sheep, and the number kept trying to slide back to what she'd last seen.

But she learned. And that is why, to this day, crows sit on fences for so long, watching, saying nothing. They are holding a number and waiting for something to leave.

*The moral, according to the tortoise: Counting tells you what was. Subtraction tells you what is. Only the second one keeps the deer fed.*
